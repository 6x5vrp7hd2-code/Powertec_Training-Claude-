Assessment · Section 13

Source Transfers and Couplings

Work through the multiple-choice and long-answer questions, then check your answers.

Multiple Choice

1. What is the approximate formula for the current that flows when paralleling two out-of- synchronisation sources?

2. Why is paralleling two feeders from the SAME busbar generally safe without a synchro- check relay?

3. What does the synchro-check relay (ANSI 25) verify before permitting a breaker to close and parallel two sources?

4. What is “reverse power” protection (ANSI 32) designed to detect?

5. What is the fundamental danger of paralleling two non-synchronised sources?

Long Answer

1. Explain, using the ΔU ÷ X relationship, why a 180° phase difference between two sources being paralleled is equivalent to a short-circuit at twice the network voltage.

2. Describe the four synchronisation conditions the guide lists, and explain which one is structural (cannot be corrected in the field) versus which are dynamically monitored during the paralleling operation.

3. Explain the difference between closed transition (make-before-break) and open transition (break-before-make) transfers, and describe the specific additional risk that closed transition introduces which open transition avoids.

4. Why does a “studied prolonged parallel” with the public utility grid require a grid operator agreement, reverse power protection, and validated combined short-circuit current, according to the guide?

5. Using the real case of an STS closed-transition bypass, explain why the MOP must always have the operator verify synchronisation before authorising the manoeuvre, even though interlocks and a synchro-check relay are already in place.

Finished? Check your answers.
