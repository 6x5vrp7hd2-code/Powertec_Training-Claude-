Assessment · Section 20

Before / During / After — The Operator’s Checklists

Work through the multiple-choice and long-answer questions, then check your answers.

Multiple Choice

1. What is a “toolbox talk” as referenced in the guide’s before-operation checklist?

2. Why must locks and earths be removed “COUNTED” at the end of an operation?

3. What is the purpose of announcing an action aloud and having it confirmed by a second participant during a MOP?

4. What should happen if an unexpected alarm occurs during a MOP execution?

5. What must be checked and restored after an operation, in addition to removing locks and earths?

Long Answer

1. Explain why the “before, during, after” checklist structure mirrors practices used in aviation and other high-reliability industries, and describe what each phase is specifically designed to catch or prevent.

2. Describe the “announce aloud, confirm” verbal protocol and explain why it is used even though the same information is already written on the MOP.

3. Explain why “any deviation = stop in a safe state + call operations” is treated as an absolute rule with no informal exceptions (“never tinker during a MOP”).

4. Describe the specific purpose of the counted-removal principle for locks and earths, and explain what failure mode it is specifically designed to catch.

5. Explain why “hot lessons learned” are captured immediately after an operation (in the after-checklist) rather than at some later, more convenient time.

Finished? Check your answers.
