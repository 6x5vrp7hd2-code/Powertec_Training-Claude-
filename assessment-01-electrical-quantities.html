Assessment · Section 01

Electrical Quantities — What to Understand Before Anything Else

Work through the multiple-choice and long-answer questions, then check your answers.

Multiple Choice

1. What does Ohm’s Law state?

2. Why is apparent power (kVA), not active power (kW), used to size cables, transformers and UPS?

3. Why does an alternating current arc extinguish more easily than a direct current arc?

4. A 500 kVA UPS operating at 0.9 power factor delivers how much real (active) power?

5. What is the main practical advantage of three-phase power distribution over single- phase for large equipment?

Long Answer

1. Explain, using Ohm’s Law, why a short-circuit produces a much larger fault current than an earth fault through soil, and why this difference requires two different families of protective device.

2. Describe what active power factor correction (PFC) does inside a modern server power supply, and explain why its widespread adoption changed data centre load-sizing practices.

3. Using the water analogy, explain the relationship between voltage, current and resistance/impedance in your own words, including why voltage can exist without any current flowing.

4. Explain why a DC arc (e.g. in a UPS battery string) is considered more dangerous to interrupt than an AC arc of the same voltage.

5. A specialist measures 400 kW of active power and 500 kVA of apparent power at an MSB feeder. Calculate the power factor and explain what this value tells them about the load.

Finished? Check your answers.
