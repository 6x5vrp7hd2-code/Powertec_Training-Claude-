Assessment · Section 14

Power Quality — Harmonics and Other Common Problems

Work through the multiple-choice and long-answer questions, then check your answers.

Multiple Choice

1. What are THDi and THDu?

2. Why do triplen harmonics (3rd, 9th, 15th…) add up in the neutral conductor instead of cancelling?

3. According to IEEE 519, why are voltage THD limits generally stricter (lower) at higher system voltages?

4. Why does an average-responding (non-TRMS) clamp meter give an unreliable reading on a harmonic-rich data centre circuit?

5. What is the recommended remedy for resonance risk when installing a tuned passive harmonic filter or PFC capacitor bank?

Long Answer

1. Explain the mechanism by which non-linear loads (UPS rectifiers, VFDs, switch-mode power supplies) generate harmonic currents, and how those currents become voltage harmonics that affect other, unrelated loads.

2. Explain why a generator supply produces a much higher THDu for the same harmonic- rich load compared to a utility supply, and what practical risks this creates.

3. Describe the IEEE 519 philosophy of “shared responsibility” between utility and end-user for harmonic control, and explain how the current-distortion limit scaling by Isc/IL ratio supports this philosophy.

4. Explain at least three of the “other classic problems” listed in the guide’s section 14.4 table (e.g., voltage sags, phase unbalance, circulating currents in the PE), including their typical cause and remedy.

5. Why does the guide warn that “a power quality measurement made on a quiet Tuesday says nothing about behaviour at the worst moment”? Describe what a proper measurement campaign should cover instead.

Finished? Check your answers.
