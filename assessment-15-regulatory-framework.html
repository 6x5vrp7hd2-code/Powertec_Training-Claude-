Assessment · Section 15

The Regulatory Framework and Electrical Qualifications

Work through the multiple-choice and long-answer questions, then check your answers.

Multiple Choice

1. What is the fundamental principle common to every regulatory framework the guide describes (French, UK, North American, and others)?

2. In the French NF C 18-510 system, what does the letter “C” (as in BC or HC) specifically designate?

3. What did the NFPA 70E 2024 edition specifically clarify regarding closed panel doors?

4. Under NFPA 70E, can the Incident Energy Analysis method and the Table (Category) method be mixed for the same piece of equipment?

5. What is a “titre d’habilitation” under the French NF C 18-510 system?

Long Answer

1. Explain why the guide states that “the texts never replace site rules,” and describe what additional layer of authorisation is always required beyond a national/regional qualification.

2. Using the French symbol system, explain the difference between a BR (LV general intervention officer) and a BC/HC (isolation officer), including what each is and is not authorised to do.

3. Describe the 2024 updates to NFPA 70E and the related OSHA rule, and explain their practical significance for a data centre operations team.

4. Explain why a “higher qualification includes the lower levels of the same domain” (e.g., a B2 can do what a B1 does) but “a B never includes an H.” What is the reasoning behind this rule?

5. Why does the guide emphasise a “country overview for a global role,” and what practical risk does it protect against for someone working across multiple countries’ data centres?

Finished? Check your answers.
