Assessment · Section 02

Conductors — Phase, Neutral, PE and Exposed Conductive Parts

Work through the multiple-choice and long-answer questions, then check your answers.

Multiple Choice

1. What is the standard IEC colour for the protective earth (PE) conductor?

2. Why is the neutral conductor considered a live, dangerous conductor even though it is a “return” path?

3. What does a PEN conductor combine?

4. In normal operation (no fault present), how much current should flow in the PE conductor?

5. What is an “exposed conductive part” (masse)?

Long Answer

1. Explain the two distinct reasons for connecting parts of an electrical installation to earth, and why they must not be confused with each other.

2. Describe what happens, step by step, if a phase conductor touches an exposed conductive part that IS properly bonded to the PE, versus one that is NOT bonded.

3. Explain why a PEN conductor is acceptable upstream of a data centre’s transformer but forbidden downstream of the UPS.

4. Using the touch-voltage figures given (human body resistance ~1,000–2,000 Ω, 230 V supply), explain how the 30 mA and 10 mA thresholds relate to real physiological danger.

5. Why does the guide say the neutral “can carry more current than the phases” is possible, and under what circumstance does this happen (cross-reference with the concept developed further in section 14)?

Finished? Check your answers.
