Structure — how it's built, and why

Stage 1

Entry Competence & Gap Analysis

Establish existing competence across HV operations, switching, proving dead and earthing, safety documents, protection, LV, UPS, generators, ATS/STS, commissioning, temporary supplies, control systems and data-centre experience.

Stage 2

Understand the Architecture Before Operating It

The overlay method, formalised. The network is reconstructed progressively across 8 overlays rather than absorbed as one complete single-line diagram.

Explore →
Stage 3

Critical-Power Systems Modules

15 modules, each with its own lesson, sources, failure modes, restoration and training strategy.

Explore →
Stage 4

Safety Rules Conversion

What you already know, what changes here, why it changes — and the discipline of always asking “what can energise this equipment?” rather than “where is its normal supply.”

Explore →
Stage 5

Structured Practical Exposure

Shadowing with defined objectives before, during and after each observation — logged as field evidence, not open-ended time on site.

Explore →
Stage 6

Progressive Competency Gates

8 formal gates. Failure at any gate identifies precisely what needs development — it doesn't send the trainee back to the beginning.

Explore →
Stage 7

Evidence Portfolio

A controlled portfolio of drawings, observed and assisted isolations, switching schedules, safety documents, commissioning activities, scenario assessments, knowledge checks and mentor reviews.

Explore →
Stage 8

Defined Development Timeline

A target readiness pathway, not a guaranteed authorisation date. Someone exceptional can progress faster; someone who hasn't demonstrated competence doesn't progress simply because time has elapsed.

Duration

There is no fixed programme length. Duration depends on current experience and knowledge as identified in the Skills Gap Analysis — an experienced HV SAP with strong LV and UPS exposure will move through the systems modules faster than someone building that knowledge for the first time. What's fixed is the sequence and the evidence required at each gate, not the calendar time it takes to produce it.

Content

15

Scenario-training modules, each with a full lesson, 4 scenarios and model answers.

21

Theory and principle modules, each with a lesson, assessment and answer key.

8

Competency gates controlling progression through the whole programme.
