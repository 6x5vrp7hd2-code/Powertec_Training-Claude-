Assessment · Section 11

The UPS — Architecture, Sources, Synchronisation and Load

Work through the multiple-choice and long-answer questions, then check your answers.

Multiple Choice

1. What does “VFI” mean in the IEC 62040-3 UPS classification?

2. Why can the static bypass switch (SSW) transfer load in only a few milliseconds?

3. What must be true of the inverter and the bypass source before a “without-break” static transfer can occur?

4. In a parallel UPS system, what happens to the total load when one module is isolated or fails?

5. Why is the MBB (maintenance bypass breaker) described as “a continuity device, not a separation device”?

Long Answer

1. Explain the three IEC 62040-3 UPS classes (VFD, VI, VFI) and why VFI (double conversion) is specified almost universally for critical data centre loads.

2. Describe why inverter-to-bypass synchronisation is described as “a capital point,” and explain what happens physically if a transfer is forced while the HMI shows “not synchronised.”

3. Explain how load sharing works in a parallel UPS system, and describe the specific risk illustrated by the guide’s example of four modules at 52% becoming three modules at 69%, then potentially becoming an overloaded two modules at 103%.

4. Describe the three (plus one) sources the guide says a UPS has, and explain why “opening both AC inputs” does not make the UPS electrically dead.

5. Explain the difference in role between the static bypass switch (SSW) and the maintenance bypass breaker (MBB), including which one is used for automatic disturbance-driven transfers versus planned maintenance.

Finished? Check your answers.
