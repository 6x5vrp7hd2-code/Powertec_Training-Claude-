Assessment · Section 21

The Red List — The Mistakes Never to Make

Work through the multiple-choice and long-answer questions, then check your answers.

Multiple Choice

1. Why is “operating a disconnector on load” the first item on the red list?

2. What is the specific danger of “closing an earthing switch without a prior voltage absence test”?

3. Why does the red list forbid “relying on the mimic, the BMS, SCADA or the VPIS to declare a circuit dead”?

4. Why is “launching an operation while redundancy is already consumed” forbidden without a formal waiver?

5. What must happen before opening a UPS cover, per the red list’s final item?

Long Answer

1. Choose three items from the red list and explain, for each, which earlier section of the guide establishes the underlying principle that the red-list item violates.

2. Explain why “bypassing, forcing or defeating an interlock” is treated as an absolute prohibition, even when a specialist believes they understand why the interlock is (in their view) unnecessarily blocking a manoeuvre.

3. Describe the specific danger of “forgetting a source” during isolation, using the UPS’s three-sources-plus-capacitors example, and explain how the isolation methodology (section 18) is designed to prevent this.

4. Explain why “carrying on despite a deviation” is on the red list, and connect this to the MOP stop-rule principle described in section 19.2 and 20.2.

5. Why does the guide describe this list as containing “the mistakes never to make,” rather than simply repeating general safety advice? What is the practical value of a short, absolute list compared to a longer set of general guidelines?

Finished? Check your answers.
