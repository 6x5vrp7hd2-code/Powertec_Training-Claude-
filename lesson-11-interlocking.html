Lesson · Module 11

Interlocking

Read this in full before attempting the scenarios below — they assume this material.

Physical enforcement versus procedural trust

Every other safety measure in this programme relies on people following a procedure correctly. Interlocking is different: a correctly functioning trapped-key or Kirk-key scheme makes certain dangerous switching sequences physically impossible, regardless of what anyone intends to do next. Understanding this distinction is the whole point of the module — an interlock isn't a reminder, it's a physical constraint.

What a specific interlock is actually preventing

A generic answer like 'it stops you doing the wrong thing' isn't useful operationally. Every interlocked scheme exists to prevent a specific, nameable dangerous configuration — commonly, two sources being closed onto a common bus while out of synchronism, or a breaker being opened while still carrying load. Being able to name the exact configuration a given interlock prevents, for the specific board in front of you, is the skill this module is testing.

A defeated interlock is a serious event, not a shortcut taken

A trapped key freed by an undocumented override, a duplicated key issued outside the normal register, or physical damage to a mechanism all have the same practical effect: the physical enforcement is gone, and the site is now relying on procedure alone for exactly the sequence the interlock existed to make impossible. This should always be treated as a significant elevation of risk requiring investigation, never as a minor administrative deviation.

Testing function, not appearance

After any interlock maintenance, key replacement, or override, a visual check that the mechanism 'looks locked' proves nothing. The only meaningful test is physically attempting each sequence the interlock is designed to block and confirming it is genuinely prevented at every stage. A key that turns and a lock that visually engages can still be the wrong profile, or sit on a damaged mechanism that no longer enforces anything.
