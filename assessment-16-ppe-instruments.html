Assessment · Section 16

Personal Protective Equipment and Instruments

Work through the multiple-choice and long-answer questions, then check your answers.

Multiple Choice

1. What is the “arc flash boundary”?

2. Under NFPA 70E, what must always be true of the arc-rated PPE selected for a task?

3. What does a CAT III rating on a multimeter indicate it is designed to withstand?

4. Why must a two-pole voltage detector (VAT), rather than a multimeter, be used for a voltage absence test?

5. What is the correct procedure (“live-dead-live”) for a voltage absence test?

Long Answer

1. Explain why the arc flash boundary is described as “not a safe line in the way a shock approach boundary implies safety,” and describe what actually happens to incident energy as distance from the source increases.

2. Describe the difference between NFPA 70E’s Incident Energy Analysis method and its Table (Category) method for selecting PPE, including a limitation of the Table method.

3. Explain the measurement category (CAT) system (CAT II, III, IV) and why using an under- rated instrument at a given point in an installation is dangerous.

4. Describe the full three-step “live-dead-live” voltage absence test protocol, and explain why skipping the final re-test on a known live source would be a serious safety gap.

5. Compare at least three PPE items from the guide’s table (e.g., insulating gloves, arc- rated clothing, insulating mat), including the standard governing each and the specific hazard each protects against.

Finished? Check your answers.
