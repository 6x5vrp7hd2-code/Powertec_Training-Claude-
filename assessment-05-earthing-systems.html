Assessment · Section 05

Earthing Systems — What Happens to the Fault Current?

Work through the multiple-choice and long-answer questions, then check your answers.

Multiple Choice

1. In the IEC 60364 two-letter code, what does the first letter describe?

2. Why is TN-S considered the standard for critical data centre distribution?

3. What is the defining risk of the TN-C system when used downstream of a UPS?

4. What happens at the first insulation fault in a well-designed IT earthing system?

5. What is “PME” (Protective Multiple Earthing), as commonly used in UK/Irish public LV distribution?

Long Answer

1. Explain, in sequence, what happens to the fault current in each of the four earthing systems (TT, TN-S, TN-C, IT) at the moment of a first insulation fault.

2. Why must an installation never be converted from TN-S back to TN-C (“only in the direction C to S”)? Explain the physical reasoning.

3. Describe why a data centre typically contains several different earthing systems simultaneously, giving at least three examples of where different systems might be used within the same site.

4. Explain the “neutral during source transfers” problem: why does a 3-pole (unswitched neutral) transfer between two earthed sources create circulating currents and nuisance ground-fault tripping in a TN-S system, and how does a 4-pole transfer solve this?

5. Compare the relative safety and practicality trade-offs of TT, TN-S, TN-C, and IT for a critical data centre load, referencing at least one advantage and one drawback of each.

Finished? Check your answers.
