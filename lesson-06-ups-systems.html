Lesson · Module 06

UPS Systems

Read this in full before attempting the scenarios below — they assume this material.

Multiple simultaneous power paths — the concept with no HV equivalent

HV switching deals with one path at a time: a circuit is either fed from this source or that one. A UPS runs multiple paths simultaneously by design — the rectifier charging the battery while the inverter supplies the load, with a static bypass path standing by in parallel. There is no single moment where 'the UPS' is definitively on one source, which is exactly why isolation planning for a UPS requires a different mental model than HV switching does.

What 'in bypass' does and doesn't tell you

When a UPS transfers to static bypass — automatically, in milliseconds, during a fault or overload — the load's dependency on the inverter is removed. Nothing else changes. The rectifier, the inverter, and critically the battery string remain exactly as energised as they were before the transfer. 'The UPS is on bypass' is a statement about the load path, not a statement about whether the UPS itself is safe to open.

Maintenance bypass as a distinct, deliberate state

Maintenance bypass is a manually operated path that removes the UPS's power electronics from the circuit entirely, and it's the only one of the UPS's operating states that makes internal work possible. Getting into and out of maintenance bypass correctly is a sequenced, monitored process — not a single switch — because an uncontrolled transfer risks a gap in supply or an inrush transient that the static bypass circuitry is specifically designed to manage during normal automatic transfers.

Why the battery is the step that's easiest to skip

Even after a UPS is correctly placed in maintenance bypass, the battery string remains a live source in its own right, connected to the DC bus until it is separately isolated. Because the load has already visibly transferred away and the front panel may show the unit as 'in bypass', it's easy to treat the job as finished at that point. The battery isolation is a distinct, necessary step — not an implied consequence of the earlier ones.
