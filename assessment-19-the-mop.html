Assessment · Section 19

The MOP — Complete Drafting Methodology

Work through the multiple-choice and long-answer questions, then check your answers.

Multiple Choice

1. What is the key difference between a MOP, an SOP, and an EOP?

2. What is a “hold point” in a MOP?

3. Why must each MOP action line contain only one imperative verb (“Open QF12,” never “open QF12 and check the load then close QF14”)?

4. Why does the guide state “if the reviewer asks a question, the MOP is incomplete”?

5. What are the six stages of the methodology for building a MOP sequence “without getting lost,” as described by the guide?

Long Answer

1. Explain the nine-part standard structure of a MOP as described by the guide, and describe why the “back-out plan” section is considered essential even for a well- planned operation.

2. Describe the six-stage methodology for building a MOP sequence, and explain why “let the interlocks dictate the order” is placed before “validation walkdown” rather than after.

3. Explain why the guide insists on imperative, single-action lines with an observable expected result for every MOP step, using an example of what NOT to write versus what TO write.

4. Describe the purpose of a “table-top / dry run” review, and explain what kind of errors this step is specifically designed to catch that a solo read-through would not.

5. Explain why the guide states that “the stop rule” (any deviation between expected and observed result = STOP) belongs to every participant, not just the work leader, and describe the practical consequence of this principle during execution.

Finished? Check your answers.
