Assessment · Section 04

Basic Protections

Work through the multiple-choice and long-answer questions, then check your answers.

Multiple Choice

1. What does an HRC fuse do when current exceeds its rated value?

2. What are the two release types normally combined inside a circuit breaker?

3. What principle underlies the operation of a residual current device (RCD)?

4. In the TT earthing system, why is an RCD not optional but mandatory?

5. What does an insulation monitoring device (IMD) do in an IT earthing system?

Long Answer

1. Explain why a fuse “is replaced, not reset” and describe the operational consequence of this for a data centre maintenance team.

2. Describe the difference between an RCD’s detection principle and a standard circuit breaker’s detection principle, and explain why this makes the RCD uniquely suited to detecting small earth faults.

3. Explain why the IMD does not trip the circuit on a first fault in an IT system, and what organisational requirement this creates for the site.

4. A PDU feeds 40 servers, each with a few milliamps of normal EMC filter leakage current. Explain why this can cause nuisance tripping of a 30 mA RCD, and why this is a key reason TT is avoided in critical IT distribution.

5. Compare the fuse, circuit breaker, RCD and IMD in terms of what fault type each is principally designed to catch.

Finished? Check your answers.
