Lesson · Module 01

HV Distribution

Read this in full before attempting the scenarios below — they assume this material.

Why HV intake design matters here

A DNO-trained SAP already understands HV switching. What's different in a data centre is the intent behind the design: intake arrangements exist specifically so that the loss of one source is invisible to the load. That only works if the two feeds are genuinely independent — different substations, different corridors, different protection zones. Reading an SLD correctly means tracing each feed all the way back to its origin, not stopping at the first RMU or ring unit that looks separate.

Shared upstream points — the hidden single point of failure

The single most common design or documentation error in multi-infeed HV intakes is two feeds that appear independent on a simplified diagram but converge on a shared breaker, shared substation, or shared overhead corridor further upstream. This doesn't show up unless you deliberately trace both feeds to their true origin. Before relying on a second feed as genuine redundancy — for isolation planning, for risk assessment, or for describing the site's resilience to a client — confirm independence rather than assume it from the SLD's layout.

DIESR in a resilient, multi-source context

Dead, Isolated, Earthed, Screened, Recorded remains the sequence regardless of how many sources exist. What changes in a data centre is the discipline required before you even reach the isolation step: you must first establish every possible source of energy for the equipment in question, not just the one currently in service. An HV circuit fed from a single DNO connection has one source to isolate. An HV circuit on a resilient, multi-infeed site may have two live sources, a standby generation path, or an automatic transfer capability that can re-energise the isolated section if not accounted for.

Reading this site's HV intake correctly

Before attempting the scenarios for this module, you should be able to point to this site's actual HV intake points on the SLD, name which substations or corridors each traces back to, and state — not assume — whether they share any common upstream point. This is exactly the skill the overlay method is built to develop: strip the HV intake down to its own diagram, trace every line back to source, and rebuild your understanding from that foundation before moving to the next overlay.
