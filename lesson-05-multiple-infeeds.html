Lesson · Module 05

Multiple Infeeds / Backfeeds

Read this in full before attempting the scenarios below — they assume this material.

The question that replaces 'where is its normal supply'

Every module in this programme returns to one discipline, and multiple infeeds is where it matters most: the question is never where a piece of equipment's normal supply comes from, it's what can possibly energise it — including standby paths, automatic transfer capability, and connections that were never formally documented. A board or item of plant with more than one incomer should be treated as a backfeed candidate by default, not as an exception to look for.

Why the 'active' infeed is a misleading concept

Labelling one infeed as 'active' and another as 'standby' describes normal running, not safety state. A standby infeed connected via an ATS or a bus-tie is still capable of energising the equipment it feeds, either through an automatic transfer event or through a fault condition. Isolating only the active infeed and treating the equipment as dead is the single most common multi-infeed isolation error, precisely because the standby path looks inactive.

Undocumented and temporary connections

Temporary connections — a generator brought in for an event, a jumper installed during commissioning — are a genuine hazard specifically because they don't appear on the SLD anyone consults during isolation planning. A connection that was never formally removed after use is, from a safety-planning point of view, indistinguishable from one that was never there — until someone finds it live during what they believed was a straightforward isolation.

Building a complete source list before you touch anything

The practical skill this module is building is the habit of listing every possible energisation source for a piece of plant before starting isolation — normal infeed, standby infeed, ATS/STS standby path, any temporary connection, and any automatic or BMS-initiated start capability — and then physically confirming each one is isolated, rather than relying on the SLD or on labels alone.
