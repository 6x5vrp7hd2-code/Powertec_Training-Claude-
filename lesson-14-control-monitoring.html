Lesson · Module 14

Control and Monitoring

Read this in full before attempting the scenarios below — they assume this material.

Information, not power — but still safety-critical

A BMS or DCIM system doesn't energise or de-energise anything on its own, but it is the primary way operators understand the state of everything else in this programme. Dismissing it as 'someone else's system' misses that its accuracy — or its failure — has direct safety consequences for anyone relying on it to understand what's live and what isn't.

Why the plant, not the screen, is always the authority

A monitoring point can fail or lose communication independently of the actual plant it's reporting on. A board correctly proved dead by physical test can still show as energised on a BMS screen if that specific monitoring point has failed. The physical proving-dead test is always the authority; the monitoring system is a convenience that has to be treated as fallible.

Treating a loss of visibility as an event in itself

Losing BMS or DCIM visibility on a cell — even with no actual change to the plant — reduces or removes real-time awareness for anyone else on site relying on that system. This should be logged and responded to as its own operational event, not dismissed as an inconvenience that will presumably resolve itself.

Closing out a permit means closing the loop with the monitoring system too

After any isolation, switching activity, or restoration, the monitoring system's displayed state should be checked against the actual, physically verified plant state before the safety document is closed out. A mismatch between the two isn't a minor discrepancy to note for later — it's a finding that needs resolving before anyone else can safely rely on that monitoring point again.
