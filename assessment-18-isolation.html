Assessment · Section 18

Isolation — From the Order of Operations to the Hand-Over

Work through the multiple-choice and long-answer questions, then check your answers.

Multiple Choice

1. What are the five steps of isolation, in order, as described by the guide?

2. What is the correct order between breaker/switch, disconnector, and earthing switch when isolating equipment?

3. Why must the third step of the “live-dead-live” voltage absence test (re-testing the detector on a known live source) never be skipped?

4. Why is earthing mandatory in MV immediately after the voltage absence test, and required in LV wherever re-energising is possible?

5. According to the guide, what must never be forgotten when separating “all possible sources” during isolation of an item like a UPS?

Long Answer

1. Explain why the isolation sequence is divided into a “first stage” (separation and locking) and later steps (identification, test for dead, earthing), and who typically performs each stage.

2. Describe the “live-dead-live” voltage absence test protocol in full, and explain the reasoning behind each of its three steps.

3. Explain why the return-to-service order must be the exact reverse of the isolation order, and describe the specific danger of reclosing a disconnector onto a closed downstream breaker.

4. Using the UPS mini-MOP example (section 18.5), explain why the note about “the MBB supply being the same cable as the UPS bypass input” is described as “the point of no return of the MOP.”

5. Compare the guide’s five-step isolation sequence with the general concept of Lock-Out Tag-Out (LOTO) as used under OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147, highlighting the structural similarities despite arising from different regulatory traditions.

Finished? Check your answers.
